# v44.0: AI Talking-Head Videos the Slow, Free Way — One Photo, One Voice Sample, an 8 GB Card, No Video Editor

> Old Mao's Local AI Engineering Notes · 2026-10-05
>
> A beginner's field guide: how we turn a few paragraphs of text into a talking presenter video — the AI reads and writes, a cloned voice speaks, one still photo comes alive — using nothing but free local tools and an 8 GB graphics card. No JianYing, no subscription, no cloud.

---

## 1. The Goal / Problem

This one is written for absolute beginners — the retired parents, the late-night tinkerers, the folks who keep asking "how do those AI presenter videos work?" Short answer: five free tools in a trench coat. Long answer: this guide.

The assumptions we are retiring:

- "You need a video editor (JianYing/CapCut) and its paid membership." — No. FFmpeg does the assembly and even burns the subtitles.
- "You need a cloud API and a bill." — No. Everything here runs locally, including the voice cloning.
- "You need a powerful computer." — No. An 8 GB RTX 5060 laptop card carries the whole chain.
- "You need to be on camera." — No. One still photo is enough.

The whole pipeline, in one line:

**text → AI writes the script → cloned voice speaks → one photo's lips move → subtitles appear → vertical video out.**

## 2. The Five Ingredients

| Ingredient | Tool | What it does | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Understands & writes | Local LLM (Qwen3.6-35B Q4_K_M) | Reads your article, writes a spoken script | free, local |
| 2. Voice cloning | CosyVoice 3.0 (300M) — or edge-tts to start | Speaks the script in a cloned or preset voice | free, local / free cloud |
| 3. Lip animation | SadTalker | Moves the mouth of one still photo to the audio | free, local |
| 4. Subtitles | FunASR (paraformer-zh) | Word-level timestamps for karaoke-style captions | free, local |
| 5. Assembly | FFmpeg | Scale, pad, burn subtitles, mux — no editor GUI | free |

Total disk: CosyVoice 5.4 GB + SadTalker a couple of GB + FunASR ~1 GB + a 35B LLM quant. Total money: zero. Total subscriptions: zero.

## 3. Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### Step 1 — The AI writes the script (the "图文理解" part)

Feed your article (with its pictures, if any) to a local chat model and ask for a read-aloud script. Our June tests with Qwen3.6-35B Q4_K_M showed what to demand from any model doing this job:

- **Sentences of 20 characters or less** — TTS aligns best on short clauses.
- **Punctuation controls the pauses** — commas and periods are your breathing marks.
- A 300-character script ≈ 1.5–2 minutes of speech. That's one video.

If you skip this step and read the text as-is, the long written sentences will make the voice sound rushed and the subtitles won't line up. Worth the 30 seconds of prompting.

### Step 2 — Clone the voice (or borrow a free one)

Two routes, both free:

- **Start here**: edge-tts preset voices (e.g. `zh-CN-XiaoxiaoNeural`), instant output, zero setup. Good enough for a first video.
- **The real thing**: CosyVoice 3.0 (300M, 5.4 GB) runs locally and clones a voice from **an 11–13 second reference clip**. We cloned two recognizable voices from short samples in our tests; the zero-shot mode needs nothing but the reference WAV and the text. One caveat from the docs of our run: the 300M zero-shot build has no built-in speaker list — you always bring a reference clip.

### Step 3 — One photo learns to talk

SadTalker takes **one still portrait** + the voice audio and renders a lip-synced clip. Our July run: a 1024×1024 portrait generated locally (SDXL-class checkpoint, 25 steps), 11.26 s of cloned audio → an 11.24 s clip at 512×512 with the mouth locked to the sound. No face melting, no video-generation model needed.

Beginner tip that cost us a day: do **not** try to make this clip with a text-to-video model first. We tried LTX-2B (an open video model) and its faces melt into color blocks after frame 12 — usable length about 0.4 s. A still photo + a dedicated lip-sync tool beats a small video model for talking heads, every time.

### Step 4 — Subtitles that follow the voice

Run FunASR (paraformer-zh) over the audio; it returns word-level timestamps at RTF 0.049 (roughly 20× faster than real time). Split lines by meaning, then burn each line with FFmpeg `drawtext` on its own time window:

```bash
FONT='/c/Windows/Fonts/simhei.ttf'
ffmpeg -i lipsync.mp4 -vf "scale=960:960,pad=1080:1920:(ow-iw)/2:(oh-ih)/2:#0F172A,\
drawtext=fontfile=${FONT}:fontsize=44:fontcolor=white:x=(w-text_w)/2:y=h-170:\
text='第一行':box=1:boxcolor=black@0.65:enable='between(t,0.15,3.25)'" out.mp4
```

Two field notes: FunASR occasionally mishears domain terms ("SadTalker" became "life portrait" in our test) — proofread the transcript; and scale the 512px face up to 960px on a 1080×1920 canvas so the presenter isn't a tiny figure in a sea of background.

### Step 5 — Assemble (this is where the video editor would have been)

That's it. The `drawtext` command above already did scale + pad + subtitles in one pass. Mux audio, export, done. No timeline GUI, no membership.

## 4. The Biggest Pitfall

**The GPU memory trap.** First run of the chain died with `CUDA illegal memory access` — the card was at 7.3 GB of 8 GB before SadTalker even started. The fix is embarrassingly simple: check what's already on the GPU and close it first. A talking-head chain is a relay race, not a group hug — run the stages one after another, never overlapping.

The second trap: our automation script hit a 600-second timeout and was killed between stages. Local chains are slow; give them real timeouts or run stages by hand the first time.

## 5. Benchmarks

All numbers from actual runs on the 8 GB card.

| Stage | Input → Output | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Script writing (Qwen3.6-35B Q4_K_M) | article → 300-char script (~2 min speech) | one chat turn |
| TTS (edge-tts) | 200-char script → audio | seconds |
| Voice clone (CosyVoice 3.0 zero-shot) | 11–13 s reference + text → cloned audio | local, 5.4 GB model |
| Lip animation (SadTalker) | 1024² photo + audio → 512² clip | ~6 min for a 46 s clip |
| Face enhance (gfpgan, optional) | 512² → sharper face | ~6 min |
| Subtitle timestamps (FunASR) | audio → word timestamps | RTF 0.049 |
| Assembly (FFmpeg) | scale + pad + burn subs + mux | seconds |

End-to-end reference point: a ~200-character news item became a **46.6 s, 8.5 MB vertical video (768×1024) in about 7 minutes** of compute, and a separate 11.24 s lip-synced demo clip with four karaoke-timed subtitle lines came out of the July pipeline.

## 6. What We Learned

1. The scary part of "AI presenter videos" is not the presenter — it's five small tools chained together. Each one is beginner-sized.
2. Short sentences (≤20 chars) are the single highest-leverage quality knob. They fix TTS rhythm *and* subtitle alignment at once.
3. One photo + a lip-sync tool beats a video-generation model for talking heads. Small video models melt faces; SadTalker just moves a mouth.
4. Voice cloning needs only 11–13 seconds of clean reference audio. That is less than a voicemail greeting.
5. Check GPU memory before you start. 8 GB is enough — but only if the relay race has one runner at a time.
6. A video editor is a convenience, not a requirement. FFmpeg's `drawtext` is the whole subtitle feature of the pipeline.

## 7. What's Next

- The chain has since grown up (our newer digital-human runs swap in H3-based animation and faster TTS) — that's for a later post. This beginner path stays valid and is the cheapest way in.
- Swap the cloned voice per character to make multi-speaker dialogues.
- Automate the relay with a single script once the stages are trusted.
