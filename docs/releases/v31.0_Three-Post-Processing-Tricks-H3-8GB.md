# v31.0: Three Post-Processing Tricks for H3 Video on 8 GB — What Works, What Doesn't, and Why

> Old Mao's Local AI Engineering Notes · 2026-09-12
>
> Motion Context gives you seamless clip-to-clip transitions (SSIM 0.96+), but what about the 1-2 seconds of face deformation mid-segment? We tested three post-processing approaches — minterpolate frame interpolation, ReActor face swapping, and their combination — with real SSIM numbers. The honest answer: each tool solves exactly one problem, and none of them overlap.

---

## 1. The Goal / Problem

[v30.0](https://github.com/Ultramanmao/ai-pipeline-local/releases/tag/v30.0) chained 24 H3 clips into a 120-second dance video using Motion Context. The segment-to-segment continuity is excellent — latent chaining produces SSIM 0.96+ between clips. But within each 5-second clip, there are pain points that generation-time techniques can't fix:

- **Face deformation during rotation** — when the character turns, H3's model struggles with simultaneous body rotation + face visibility. The face distorts for 1-2 seconds mid-clip.
- **Motion stuttering** — at 24fps, fast movements (sleeve flicks, turns) can look jerky, especially in dance sequences.
- **Identity drift across clips** — even with Motion Context, the face may subtly shift over 24 clips.

The question: can post-processing fix these, or are they baked into the model? We tested three approaches on the same 5-second clip (a dancer performing an arabesque turn) to find out.

## 2. The Three Approaches

| Approach | What It Targets | Processing Time | GPU Required |
|----------|----------------|-----------------|--------------|
| ffmpeg minterpolate | Motion smoothness (段内平滑) | ~2 min/segment | No (CPU only) |
| ReActor face swap | Face identity consistency | ~1.5 min/segment | Yes |
| Selective ReActor | Only the deformed portion | ~0.5 min/segment | Yes |

**Key insight from the start:** these three tools address completely different problems. They don't compete — they compose.

## 3. Approach 1: minterpolate — Smooth Motion, No GPU

### What It Does

ffmpeg's minterpolate filter generates intermediate frames using motion estimation. Upsample from 24fps to 60fps, then sample back to 24fps — the result is smoother motion trajectories.

```bash
ffmpeg -i clip.mp4 -vf "minterpolate=fps=60:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:me_mode=bidir:vsbmc=1" \
  -r 24 -c:v libx264 -crf 10 clip_smooth.mp4
```

### Results

| Metric | Original | minterpolate | Delta |
|--------|----------|--------------|-------|
| File size | 625 KB | 937 KB | +50% |
| Bitrate | 907 kbps | 1374 kbps | +50% |
| Processing time | 0 | ~2 min | CPU-only |
| Motion smoothness | Jerky sleeves | Smooth trajectories | ✅ Visible improvement |
| Face quality | Same | Same | ❌ No change |

The +50% bitrate is expected — minterpolate generates new pixel data for the intermediate frames. The improvement is visible in fast movements: sleeve flicks, turns, and footwork become noticeably smoother.

### What It Cannot Do

minterpolate is a motion estimator, not a structural fix. If the face is deformed at t=3s (五官扭曲, 轮廓模糊), interpolating frames around it just produces more frames of a deformed face. It smooths *motion trajectories*, not *structural quality*.

## 4. Approach 2: ReActor — Face Identity Swap

### What It Does

ReActor (inswapper_128.onnx + YOLOv5n detector) replaces the face in each frame with a reference face. It solves identity consistency — making sure the same character appears across all clips.

```python
# ComfyUI ReActor node parameters
# Input: clip2.mp4 (5s, turn sequence with face deformation at t=4.9s)
# Reference: tang_dancer_arabesque.png (clean frontal face)
# Model: inswapper_128.onnx
# Detector: YOLOv5n
```

### Full-Segment Swap Results

| Timepoint | Original Face Quality | ReActor SSIM | Notes |
|-----------|----------------------|--------------|-------|
| t=0.0s | 9/10 ✅ | 0.955 | Side profile, clean |
| t=2.5s | 9/10 ✅ | 0.949 | Turning back to frontal |
| t=4.9s | 2/10 ❌ | 0.943 | **Original severely deformed, ReActor version keeps identity** |

Processing time: ~1.5 minutes for a 5-second clip.

### The Critical Distinction

ReActor solves **identity consistency** (the face *looks like* the right person), NOT **structural quality** (the face *is anatomically correct*).

When H3 generates a turning figure and the face deforms at t=4.9s, the underlying face structure is wrong — distorted proportions, blurred features. ReActor pastes a clean reference face onto this deformed base. The result: the identity is correct, but the face sits on a distorted head structure. At extreme angles, detection rate drops further.

### Selective Swap (Only the Deformed Segment)

Instead of swapping the entire clip, swap only the frames where the face deforms:

| Version | File Size | Processing Time | Description |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|-------------|
| Original | 625 KB | 0 | As generated |
| Full ReActor | 581 KB | ~1.5 min | All frames swapped |
| Selective ReActor | 651 KB | ~0.5 min | 0-3s original + 3-5s swapped |

The selective approach saves 1 minute per clip and preserves the clean original frames where no fix is needed.

## 5. Approach 3: Combining All Three

The production pipeline for quality-critical segments:

```
H3 Generation (Motion Context chain)
    ↓
minterpolate (段内平滑, CPU, +2min/段)
    ↓
Selective ReActor (只换变形段, GPU, +0.5min/段)
    ↓
Final Video
```

### Cost-Benefit Summary

| Fix | What It Solves | Cost | What It Doesn't Fix |
|-----|---------------|------|---------------------|
| Motion Context (generation-time) | Clip-to-clip continuity | Already in pipeline | Mid-clip face deformation |
| minterpolate (post-processing) | Motion smoothness | +2 min/segment, CPU-only | Face quality, identity |
| ReActor (post-processing) | Face identity | +0.5-1.5 min/segment | Structural face deformation |
| Vertical crop (post-processing) | Hide hand/edge deformities | 0 cost (just crop) | Doesn't fix, just hides |

## 6. The Honest Assessment

### What Actually Improved

- **Motion smoothness**: minterpolate made dance movements noticeably smoother. Worth the 2 minutes for action-heavy segments.
- **Face identity across clips**: ReActor kept the same face across 24 clips. Useful for narrative content where character recognition matters.

### What Didn't Improve

- **Face deformation during turns**: This is an H3 model-level limitation. ReActor pastes a clean face onto a distorted base structure. The underlying head proportions, neck angle, and feature positions are still wrong. No amount of post-processing can fix a fundamentally wrong 3D pose estimate.
- **Hand deformities**: 10 of 24 clips had mangled fingers. None of these tools fix hand structure. Vertical cropping is the only practical workaround on 8GB.

### The Real Solutions (Not Post-Processing)

| Solution | Feasibility | Effectiveness |
|----------|------------|---------------|
| Accept it + vertical crop | ✅ Free | Hides edge deformities |
| Multi-seed scanning | ✅ +30 min/segment | Sometimes avoids the bad roll |
| SCAIL-2 / Wan2.1 ControlNet | ❌ Needs 16GB+ | Would fix pose-guided generation |
| H3 FaceRefine node | ⚠️ Untested | May help with face structure |

**Bottom line:** post-processing is polish, not repair. The generation step determines quality ceiling. On 8GB hardware with H3, the practical strategy is: generate the best you can with Motion Context + 20 steps + beta scheduler, smooth the motion with minterpolate, crop vertically to hide the edges, and accept that 1-2 seconds of face wobble per 5-second clip is the current hardware limit.

## 8. What We Learned

1. **minterpolate and ReActor solve different problems.** Don't choose between them — they compose. minterpolate handles motion, ReActor handles identity. Neither handles structural quality.
2. **Selective ReActor saves time without quality loss.** If only 2 seconds of a 5-second clip have face issues, swapping the entire clip wastes time and risks introducing artifacts in the clean frames.
3. **Post-processing has a hard ceiling.** If the generation step produces a structurally deformed face, no post-processing can reconstruct correct 3D geometry. The fix must happen at generation time (ControlNet, pose guidance, better models).
4. **+50% bitrate from minterpolate is acceptable.** The smoothness improvement is visible and the file size increase is modest.
5. **SSIM numbers tell the real story.** ReActor SSIM 0.943 on a deformed frame sounds good, but visual inspection shows it's a clean face on a wrong base. Always check both metrics and eyes.

## What's Next
