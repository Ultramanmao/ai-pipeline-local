# v43.0: Qwen-Image-2.1 Prompt Enhancer on 8 GB — 11 Minutes per Prompt, and the Transparency Trap

> Old Mao's Local AI Engineering Notes · 2026-10-05
>
> We tested whether Qwen-Image-2.1-PE-I2I should write our image-editing prompts for us. It writes brilliant prompts — and it silently changes "transparent background" into "white background", at 11.4 minutes per instruction on an 8 GB RTX 5060.

---

## 1. The Goal / Problem

Our Qwen-Image-2.1 pipeline (INT8 ConvRot, released in v35) leans on a handwritten prompt template whenever we extract a subject with a transparent background. It works, but the template is thin on visual detail: the model is told "extract the woman", not *which* woman, wearing what, holding what.

Qwen-Image-2.1-PE-I2I promises to fix exactly this: a 9B VLM rewrites a lazy instruction ("cut out the lady, transparent background") into a precise, image-grounded edit prompt — it looks at the reference image and names every detail. On paper this is a perfect fit for our batch matting and composite work.

So we ran a controlled A/B test: does PE-I2I produce *better edit prompts* than our handwritten template, and is it worth its runtime on 8 GB hardware?

## 2. The Three Arms

Same reference image (896×1184 portrait, woman in a white patterned qipao holding a white oil-paper umbrella), same seed (42), same generation stack, three prompt sources.

| Arm | Prompt source | What it tests |
|---|---|---|
| A | Handwritten RGBA template | Our current SOP |
| B | PE-I2I rewrite, verbatim | The tool's raw output |
| C | Hybrid: RGBA template + PE detail | The practical candidate |

Shared stack: `qwen_image_2.1_int8_convrot` UNET + `qwen3vl_8b_int8_convrot` text encoder + `qwen_image_2.1_vae_bf16`, KSampler 25 steps / cfg 4.0 / euler / simple, resolution 1024. PE-I2I itself: `qwen3.5_9b_qwen_image_2.1_pe_i2i.int8_convrot.safetensors` (9,471,072,252 bytes, ConvRot INT8), loaded through the stock `CLIPLoader` (`type=qwen_image`) and driven by the community node `QwenImage21_EditPromptRewrite` (seed 42, `presence_penalty 0` as its docs require for the edit variant).

Hardware honesty: 8 GB RTX 5060, with Wan2GP co-resident holding ~5.3 GB during the test. Everything below ran under real memory pressure, not on an idle card.

## 3. Arm A: The Handwritten Template

Our template is three sentences: *"This is an RGBA image with transparency. Extract the woman from the reference image. The image has alpha channel and the background is transparent."*

Result: 90.6 s of sampling. The alpha channel exists — 76% of pixels fully transparent — but the subject is a ghost: only **1,533 pixels** out of 253K subject pixels are fully opaque. Composited onto anything, the woman comes out semi-transparent. The vision check added two more flaws: a white halo with aliasing along the umbrella rim, and over-smoothed hair edges.

The template gets the *mode* right and the *content* wrong.

## 4. Arm B: The PE-I2I Rewrite

PE-I2I got one lazy Chinese instruction: "cut the lady out of `<image1>`, make the background transparent." In 681.8 seconds it returned a prompt that reads like a photo shoot brief:

> ...extract the woman and the oil-paper umbrella she holds with both hands... keep the black hair, pearl drop earrings, the turning-back smile, the white standing-collar qipao with subtle jacquard weave and side slit, the cream umbrella with bamboo ribs and black handle... completely remove the moon gate, stone path, shrubs, rocks, trees... **replace the entire background with pure white**...

Two observations:

1. **The visual grounding is real.** It named the jacquard weave, the side slit, the bamboo ribs, the moon gate in the background — all correct, all from looking at the image. That is exactly the detail our template lacks.
2. **It changed the task.** We asked for a transparent background. It prescribed a pure white one — and reported `parse_ok: true` while doing it. PE-I2I has no concept of Qwen-Image-2.1's RGBA generation mode; "transparent" is not a word in its edit vocabulary, so it substituted the nearest visual equivalent it *can* express.

Following its own prescription, Arm B produced the best-looking image of the three: razor-clean edges, every fabric detail preserved, zero artifacts — with a fully opaque alpha channel and a baked-in white background. As a cutout, it is a failure. As a white-background image, it is beautiful.

## 5. Arm C: The Hybrid

For Arm C we prepended the RGBA template and made exactly two surgical corrections to PE's text ("replace with pure white" → "replace with fully transparent", "fill the background with pure white" → "background fully transparent").

Result: 79.8 s, and the alpha channel is correct — **246,891 fully-opaque subject pixels** (23% of frame) with a proper soft alpha gradient at the edges. Detail retention matches Arm B. The one flaw: a purple matte fringe along the hair (the RGB under semi-transparent edge pixels carries the generator's purple placeholder color — a classic un-premultiplied alpha trap when compositing onto light backgrounds).

**Arm C is the practical winner**: correct mode, PE-grade detail, one fixable fringe artifact.

## 6. The Transparency Trap

The trap, stated plainly: **PE-I2I is mode-blind.** It will confidently rewrite a mode-critical instruction into a different mode and report success. Any pipeline that feeds PE output straight into a generation node inherits this silently — Arm B looks like a flawless generation while having failed the actual job.

The fix is boring and cheap: keep the mode template (RGBA/alpha wording) handwritten and let PE write only the *content* prescription, with a mode-corrector pass over its output. PE writes better nouns; it must not be allowed to write your mode.

## 7. The Twist: Is White-Background-Then-Cutout Easier?

Arm B's edges were so clean that a new route suggested itself: generate on white, then cut the white away in post — a chroma-key-grade trivial operation, right?

We tested it on CPU against the Arm C alpha as reference (subject = 253,149 px). It is the worst case by construction: a white subject on a white background.

| Method | Time | Subject recall | Precision | Visual result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Naive white threshold (min > 235, max−min < 12) | <1 s | **0.747** | 0.998 | White qipao and umbrella eaten away — visible holes and broken fabric |
| GrabCut (rect init, 5 iterations) | 3.7 s | **0.984** | 0.992 | Subject intact; hair turns into hard blocks, umbrella rim jagged, one light residue patch behind the head |

The "chroma-level simple" claim is falsified for white subjects: a quarter of the subject vanishes. GrabCut rescues the subject but the matting step gives back every edge-quality advantage the white-background generation had won — hard edges and residue, versus Arm C's natural alpha gradient plus a fringing artifact.

## 8. The Honest Assessment

**What Actually Improved**

- PE-I2I's visual grounding is genuinely better than our template: correct fabric, props, and scene details with zero manual description.
- The hybrid form (template + PE content + mode correction) beats both pure arms on the metric that matters — a correct alpha with full detail.

**What Didn't Improve**

- Runtime. 681.8 s per rewrite (first call, including the 9.47 GB model load under memory pressure) against a 2-minute bar for SOP adoption. Writing the template takes 30 seconds.
- Mode compliance. Unusable output without correction whenever the task involves transparency.
- The white-background shortcut. Dead on white subjects without an ML-grade matting step.

**Real Solutions**

| Solution | Feasibility | Effectiveness |
|---|---|---|
| PE-I2I for routine prompt writing | Low (11.4 min/instruction) | High quality, wrong mode |
| PE-I2I for batch/high-value edit instructions | Medium (amortize over batches, idle GPU) | High |
| Handwritten template + PE content + mode fix (Arm C) | High | Correct alpha + full detail |
| Generate white-bg + naive threshold | High | Fails on white subjects (recall 0.747) |
| Generate white-bg + GrabCut | High | Recall 0.984 but edge quality regresses |

## 10. What We Learned

1. A rewrite tool without a mode vocabulary will translate your mode into its nearest visual concept and report success. Pin mode-critical wording yourself.
2. "More detailed prompt" is not the same axis as "correct alpha" — Arm B won detail and failed the job in the same generation.
3. Fully-opaque pixel count is a one-line, objective alpha quality check. It caught Arm A's ghosting that eyeballing missed.
4. Post-cutout on a generated white background re-pays the edge-quality bill the generation had already settled. Direct RGBA (hybrid form) stays the default route.
5. A 9.47 GB prompt-rewriter on an 8 GB card that is already hosting a 5.3 GB workload = 11 minutes per call. Benchmark your helpers; they live on the same GPU as your workload.

## 11. What's Next

- Batch-mode PE-I2I: queue a night of complex edit instructions against idle GPU, cache the rewrites.
- Fringe cleanup pass for Arm C's purple hair edge (un-premultiplied alpha correction).
- The white-bg + GrabCut route gets a second chance only with SAM/rembg-grade matting, for non-white subjects.

## Summary

**PE-I2I writes the best prompts on the machine and the wrong mode every time — keep the mode handwritten, let it write the detail, and only at 11 minutes a prompt is it worth the wait.**
