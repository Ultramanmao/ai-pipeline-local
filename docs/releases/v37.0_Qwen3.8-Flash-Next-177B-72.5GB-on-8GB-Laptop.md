**TL;DR:** A 72.5 GB / 176.94B-parameter MoE (UD-IQ1_S, 1.5625 bpw nominal) runs on a 32 GB RAM + 8 GB VRAM laptop at **6.11 tok/s** with **56/60 on a six-dimension ability suite and zero hallucinated digits in vision OCR** — the 1-bit quality collapse we documented on 27B models did not repeat. ## 1. The Question

72.5 GB of model weights. 8 GB of VRAM. 32 GB of RAM. Every intuition says this needs a datacenter — or at least a "must-crash" `WinError 1455` when Windows runs out of commit. We wanted to know what actually happens, and we wanted the answer to be unhackable by hindsight: the test plan (2026-09-25, `Qwen3.8-Flash-Next_UD-IQ1_S测试方案_v1.md`) pinned all predictions and decision rules in writing before a single benchmark ran.

Three risks were on the table:

1. **Memory**: the commit limit started at 47.3 GB — less than the 72.5 GB file. Not enough pagefile = instant death.
2. **Speed**: dense 27B Q4 models do 3–30 t/s here. What does 6.5× the weights do?
3. **Quality**: our own v-record shows IQ1_S on a 27B collapsing (digit hallucinations, repetition). Would 1-bit quantization eat a 177B model too?

## 2. What "177B" Actually Means (three calibers, pinned)

One model, three parameter counts floating around the internet (176B / 125B / 512x56B). We pinned all three by cross-referencing GGUF metadata (67 KV pairs, shard 00001), the HF model card, and per-tensor shape sums. **Don't add these numbers together — they are different accounting layers:**

| Caliber | Value | Source | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Total parameters** | **176.94 B** | tensor-shape sum (112.16B + 64.79B) | what "177B" refers to |
| Language model | 125 B (6 B active) | HF model card | "125B-A6B" = the LM proper |
| n-gram embeddings | 51 B (20M bigram/trigram, looked up at layer 2) | model card + `per_layer_token_embd`/PLE tensors | a big slice of the total, but only a few rows queried per token — "offloadable parameter extension" |
| MTP head | 4 B (1 layer; 1.3–1.7× optional speedup, not tested here — see v36.0) | HF model card | — |
| `general.size_label` | 512x56B | GGUF metadata | expert layout label (512 experts), **not** a parameter count |

Architecture (qwen4exp / Qwen4 preview): 48 layers = 12×(3×(Gated DeltaNet→MoE) + 1×(QSA sparse attention→MoE)); 512 experts, 10 routed + 1 shared active, expert FFN 640; Gated Residual with 4 branches; 256K native context (ext. 1M); vision encoder included (mmproj-F16, 904 MB).

Quantization: **UD-IQ1_S** — unsloth dynamic quant + imatrix (926-sample calibration), measured density 0.41 B/param (1.5625 bpw nominal), **67.55 GiB** on disk.

## 3. Phase 0 — Defusing the Commit-Limit Bomb

Windows reports a commit limit of 47.3 GB against a 72.5 GB file. The folklore says: expand pagefile to cover the model or die. We verified instead of assuming.

**Setup:** pagefile set via registry to `16384 98304` (16–96 GB), 32.78 GB physical RAM. Then a **commit pressure probe** (`commit_ceiling_probe.py`): `VirtualAlloc MEM_COMMIT` in steps — commits charge the limit but never touch pages, and can be released gracefully at any time.

| Cumulative commit | Pagefile allocated | Commit limit | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 20 GB | 16 GB | 47.3 GB | OK |
| 45 GB | 27.6 GB | 59.1 GB | OK (pagefile starts growing) |
| 75 GB | 57.6 GB | 89.0 GB | OK |
| 105 GB | 87.6 GB | **118.9 GiB** | OK (can grow to ~127 GB) |

**Verdict: a min/max custom pagefile grows on demand.** The "must crash" red line disappears once the registry cap is high enough — no pre-allocation, no reboot rituals. A probe tells you more in 30 seconds than staring at Task Manager ever will.

## 7. Pitfalls

1. **ngl is not "bigger is better".** 20 layers is *slower* than pure CPU (3.11 vs 5.66 t/s). For a 72 GB model on an 8 GB card the sweet spot is single-digit layers; a 3-point gradient test (0/8/20) finds it in one run.
2. **mmap makes a 72.5 GB model load in minutes.** The "10–20 minutes per load" fear assumes a full read; all three llama-bench configs ran in ~10 minutes total.
3. **Pagefile min/max custom is enough — no pre-allocation ritual.** Windows grows it on demand; the commit probe (Section 3) is the direct way to know.
4. **Vision tests need archived ground truth.** What's labeled on a chart is provable from the generator script. Our own first visual check got this wrong.
5. **Raw `/completion` EOS-bombs this model.** It instantly emits EOS (3,023 s of prefill wasted); always use `/v1/chat/completions`.
6. **MTP head GPU offload crashes** (`invalid vector subscript`) — `--spec-draft-ngl 0` is the workaround (deep dive in v36.0).
7. **Stock llama.cpp has no `graph_mtp@qwen4exp`** — MTP on this model requires the unsloth mix build.
8. **`--reasoning off --jinja`** (llama-server) is the clean way to disable thinking — 8/8 answers had zero CoT residue.

## 8. The Honest Limitations

- Speed: tg64 short generation × 3 repeats only. Long-context growth, multi-turn KV slowdown not measured (the 128K slot test is a separate, narrow probe).
- Ability: 1 question per dimension; self-graded with subjective components (hard facts independently verified). Tool-calling / agentic ability untested.
- Vision: 2 + 4 items total — not a multimodal benchmark.
- MTP: measured at 1.10× only; DFlash is MoE-incompatible.
- Sampling was non-greedy (temp 0.7), single measurement. The quality verdict is "no collapse observed", not a strict benchmark.
- Speeds are warm-cache; cold/random-read workloads not separately tested.

## 9. What We Learned

1. **The Windows commit limit is a moving ceiling, not a wall** — registry pagefile min/max + a VirtualAlloc probe replaces superstitious reboot rituals.
2. **On over-committed models, the ngl curve is inverted-U**: a handful of layers on GPU helps, more layers hurts. Test 0/8/20 before believing any "full offload" advice.
3. **1-bit quality collapse is not a law of IQ1_S** — at 177B with imatrix calibration it didn't reproduce. But n=small; treat as "not observed", not "impossible".
4. **Pre-registration works.** Predictions locked the day before, then hit 4/4 — it's the cheapest credibility tool in local benchmarking.
5. **A 176.94B model with 6B active parameters is genuinely interactive at 6.11 t/s** — which makes "quality for batch/offline work" a real option on a laptop.
