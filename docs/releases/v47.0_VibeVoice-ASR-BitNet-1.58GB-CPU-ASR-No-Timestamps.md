# v47.0: VibeVoice-ASR-BitNet on CPU: 1.58 GB and RTF 0.375 — and the Timestamps That Never Show Up

> Date: 2026-11-16 · CPU: AMD Ryzen 7 H 260, 16 threads · GPU: RTX 5060 Laptop 8 GB (occupied by a render all test — never touched) · Runtime: VibeASR.cpp (ggml, MinGW-w64) · OS: Windows 11 native
>
> Release: https://github.com/Ultramanmao/ai-pipeline-local/releases/tag/v47.0

**TL;DR:** Microsoft's 1.58 GB BitNet ASR is genuinely real-time on a laptop CPU (RTF 0.375 at 8 threads on 116 s of audio) and its whole-file accuracy beats our FunASR subtitle chain (CER 2.84% vs 4.58%). But the "who spoke when" capability that made the headlines belongs to the 7 B sibling — the BitNet build emits **no timestamps at all**, and on long audio it deterministically drops the tail (6 of 31 sentences gone). It is a good offline text-transcription emergency backup. It is not a subtitle-timestamp replacement, and we have the 31-sentence A/B to prove both halves.

---

## 1. The Goal / Problem

Our video pipeline burns sentence-level subtitles from a FunASR chain (paraformer-zh + VAD), and that chain runs with local compute we already pay for. A daily-briefing lead pointed at `microsoft/VibeVoice-ASR-BitNet`: "ASR at 1.58 GB, CPU real-time (RTF < 1), plus streaming ASR that tells you who spoke when."

Two questions, in order of importance:

1. **Can it replace the FunASR subtitle chain?** That means sentence-level timestamps, not just text. Subtitles without timestamps are just a transcript.
2. **If not, is the text quality good enough to matter?** The GPU on this machine is usually busy rendering video; an ASR that runs on CPU at RTF < 1 has a real niche as an offline transcription backup.

Test material: one 116.1 s Chinese TTS narration (24 kHz mono, 31 sentences, single speaker) with a known reference text — the same clip our FunASR chain already processed, so baseline numbers were on the table before the first run. Machine: AMD Ryzen 7 H 260 (16 threads) laptop, Windows 11, **CPU only** — the 8 GB GPU was busy with a video render for the entire test and was never touched.

## 2. The Three Modes

| Mode | What it targets | Wall time (116.1 s audio) | GPU |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. BitNet whole-file | Transcript with full context | 43.5 s (8 T) / 69.9 s (4 T) | No |
| B. BitNet per-sentence (energy VAD split) | Transcript + timestamps via VAD | 45.1 s aggregate (4 T) | No |
| C. FunASR chain (baseline, in production) | Subtitles with timestamps | Runs on GPU/local | Yes |

## 3. Mode A: Whole-File — Great Text, Gone Tail

One call over the full clip, greedy decoding:

```bash
asr_infer --vae-model vibeasr-vae-encoder-i8_s.gguf \
          --lm-model vibeasr-lm-i2_s-embed-q6_k.gguf \
          --audio narration.wav -t 8 --warmup --greedy
```

Results: **CER 2.84%** against the reference — better than the FunASR baseline's 4.58%. The 388 characters it did cover (sentences 1–25 of 31) were fully aligned.

Then the catch. Greedy decoding **stops early, deterministically**: sentence 25 of 31 is the last one out. Roughly 21 s of audio (the final 6 sentences) never gets transcribed. This reproduces bit-for-bit — 4-thread and 8-thread outputs are identical — so it is model behavior (early EOG on long single-pass generation), not a configuration problem. We did not dig into logits to confirm the mechanism; that attribution is an inference from the observed behavior.

`--prompt-format json` does not help. Over the whole file it truncates even harder (93 tokens out), and long audio + JSON prompt tends to produce empty output. Asking for JSON with `--context` makes the whole thing emit nothing.

## 4. Mode B: Per-Sentence — Timestamps for Free, Accuracy for a Price

If the model will not emit timestamps, something else has to. We split on energy VAD (20 ms frames, 10 ms hop, RMS threshold 335.8, merge gaps ≤ 0.3 s) which cut the clean TTS audio into **31 speech islands = exactly 31 sentences**, then ran BitNet per island with `--greedy`. Timestamps come from the VAD — clearly labeled as VAD-derived, not model output.

Timestamp quality against the FunASR chain on the same audio:

| Check | VAD + BitNet | FunASR chain |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence-start deviation (n=31) | mean abs 0.050 s, max 0.154 s | reference |
| Last sentence end | 115.02 s | 115.37 s (audio: 116.10 s) |

All 31 sentence starts agree with the FunASR chain to within 0.154 s.

Text quality, however, collapses: **CER 20.83%** (median 18.2%; 6 sentences zero-error, 1 sentence empty). Without cross-sentence context the decoder wrecks proper nouns and numbers — out-of-vocabulary product names (MiniCPM, FastH3, colibri, DSpark and friends) come back as wrong characters, and a numeric token like "19B/35B" turns into a run of mangled digits. Same model, same audio: 2.84% with context vs 20.83% without. Context is worth 7× accuracy here.

## 5. The Honest Assessment

### What Actually Improved

- **CPU real-time is real**: RTF 0.3745 (8 threads) on a laptop CPU. The official figure (0.37 at 4 threads on an i7-13700) is the same ballpark; our mobile chip lands between that and the EPYC numbers.
- **Whole-file text quality beats the production chain where it covers**: CER 2.84% vs FunASR's 4.58% — note BitNet's number only spans sentences 1–25; the tail is gone (below).
- **Timestamps via VAD are usable**: 0.050 s mean deviation from the FunASR sentence starts is well inside subtitle tolerance.
- **The model identity checks out**: `microsoft/VibeVoice-ASR-BitNet` on HF (MIT, arXiv:2607.21075), two ready-to-use GGUFs totaling 1,695,957,664 bytes = 1.58 GiB — the "1.58 GB" claim is accurate. The LM decoder is a Qwen2.5-1.5B; the pair is a 7 B pipeline compressed down (4.62 GB FP16 → 1.58 GB).

### What Didn't Improve

- **No timestamps, anywhere.** Whole-file, 30 s chunks, 3 s single sentences — all three lengths tested against `--prompt-format json`: output is plain text or empty. Never Start/End/Speaker/Content. The official HF Space demo also returns plain text per chunk. The structured "who spoke when" JSON is a 7 B sibling capability (the runtime's prompt_builder source even tags it "json" format (7B model...); the model card never promises timestamps for BitNet — the absence is inference from source comments plus exhaustive testing, not from a training config).
- **Tail truncation on long audio.** Deterministic loss of the last 6/31 sentences in single-pass mode.
- **Per-sentence mode destroys accuracy** (20.83% CER) unless you feed context back in.
- **The JSON parser silently eats what it cannot parse.** `asr_infer` only accepts compact `{"Key":...}` objects; JSON with spaces gets swallowed with no error. We only found this by dumping raw output from a patched scratch copy.

### Real Solutions

| Solution | Feasibility | Effectiveness |
|---|---|---|
| VAD split + BitNet per island (Mode B) | Done in this test | Timestamps OK, text poor (20.8% CER) |
| Silence-boundary chunking (~30 s) + full context per chunk | Official demo's own approach | Best expected text; block-offset timestamps approximate |
| `asr_stream_server` (official streaming server) | Ready in the runtime | Solves truncation; keeps model loaded (saves 1.3 s/call) |
| Drop-in replacement for FunASR subtitle chain | **No** | Timestamp dimension absent from the model |
| Run the 7 B sibling for real JSON timestamps | Not here | 4.62 GB FP16 + KV does not fit alongside an 8 GB GPU's video workload |

## 6. Benchmarks

RTF = processing time / audio duration, model load (~1.27 s) excluded. Input: the same 116.1 s clip in every row.

| Run | Threads | Request time | RTF | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Whole-file (text) | 4 | 69.85 s | **0.6017** | VAE encode 53.1 s (acoustic 26.9 + semantic 26.3), prefill 6.17 s/919 tok, decode 10.43 s/335 tok @ 31.1 ms/tok |
| Whole-file (text) | 8 | 43.49 s | **0.3745** | VAE 30.2 s, prefill 3.39 s, decode 9.82 s @ 29.3 ms/tok |
| Whole-file (json prompt) | 4 | 62.83 s | 0.5412 | Truncated at 93 tok — low reference value |
| Per-sentence (31 clips) | 4 | 45.1 s aggregate | **0.3886** | 73.2 s including per-run load; per-sentence RTF mean 0.478 (0.376–0.528) |

4 → 8 threads scales 1.6×. The dual VAE encoder (acoustic + semantic) is ~76% of the runtime — LM decode is not the bottleneck; chunking long audio attacks the actual bottleneck.

Official reference for comparison (20 s clips, steady state incl. warmup, their hardware): EPYC 7V13 4T = 0.46 / 8T = 0.27; i7-13700 4T = 0.37 / 8T = 0.33.

Character error rate (punctuation/whitespace/case normalized edit distance vs reference):

| Mode | CER | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| FunASR chain (per-sentence clips) | **4.58%** | Production baseline, 31 sentences |
| BitNet whole-file | **2.84%** | Covers sentences 1–25 (388 chars aligned); tail 6 sentences truncated away |
| BitNet per-sentence (VAD) | **20.83%** | Median 18.2%; 6 clean sentences, 1 empty; proper nouns/numbers collapse |

## 7. Build & Download Pitfalls

The model is a 1.58 GB download; the engineering tax was elsewhere. All of this was hit on Windows:

1. **Windows build accepts MinGW GCC only.** VibeASR.cpp's CMake explicitly rejects MSVC. No gcc on the box → pulled niXman mingw-builds 16.2 (107.9 MB .7z). No 7z.exe either → `uv run --with py7zr` to unpack.
2. **`git submodule clone` crawled at ~260 B/s.** Pulled a fixed-commit tarball of `3rdparty/llama.cpp` through gh-proxy.com and filled the directory by hand. Three symlinks in the tarball cannot be created under MSYS; none are on the build path.
3. **Download throttling everywhere.** hf-mirror and huggingface.co both sat at ~60 KB/s single-connection (network congestion day); self-written 32-way Range-block downloader (pget.py) reached ~1 MB/s. Note: gh-proxy.com honors Range requests, codeload.github.com does not (and sat at 7 KB/s).
4. **A stale background curl can overwrite finished files.** An earlier single-connection curl was still slowly writing the same GGUF name and would have truncated the LM file hours later. Fixed by `os.chmod(S_IREAD)` on finished files. The VAE file (curl holding the name) was stored as `...gguf.dl` — content complete, GGUF magic verified.
5. **Sampling hallucinates; greedy does not.** At temperature 0.1 the decoder opened with unrelated chatter. Every result in this article is `--greedy`, which is also why the truncation reproduces bit-for-bit.
6. **Native tools don't accept MSYS `/c/` paths** (ffmpeg bit us again). Use `C:/` style everywhere.
7. **Long audio + JSON prompt = empty/short output**, and `--context` injection makes whole-file output empty outright (early EOG).

## 8. What We Learned

1. **Check the model card against the sibling's feature list before believing a headline.** "Who spoke when" is true of the 7 B VibeVoice-ASR. The 1.58 GB BitNet build is a different product with a subset of the skills — and the runtime's own source comments say so.
2. **Context is not a nice-to-have for ASR decoding; it is 7× of accuracy** (2.84% vs 20.83% CER on identical audio). Any per-chunk strategy must keep chunks large enough for names and numbers to have context, or keep a correction pass.
3. **When a model claims JSON output, verify with raw dumps.** A parser that silently discards what it cannot parse turns a format question into a phantom "model outputs nothing" bug.
4. **Energy VAD is a legitimate timestamp source if you label it honestly.** 0.050 s mean deviation from a full ASR chain is fine for subtitles on clean speech — but it is VAD geometry, not recognition.
5. **The bottleneck was not where we expected.** VAE encoding is ~76% of the runtime; LM decode is cheap. The optimization lever for long audio is chunking the encoder input, not faster decoding.
6. **Greedy beats temperature 0.1 for evaluation runs** — determinism made the truncation bug reproducible and the 4 T/8 T comparison exact.

## 9. What's Next

- Keep the FunASR chain as the subtitle authority. BitNet stays in the toolbox as the no-GPU text transcription fallback (whole-file or ≤30 s silence-boundary chunks with context).
- If a project ever needs "who spoke when" on CPU: wait for timestamps to land in the BitNet build, or budget GPU for the 7 B sibling — not possible while the 8 GB card is rendering.
- Optional housekeeping: keep `asr_stream_server` resident to save the 1.3 s load per call; chunk long audio before the VAE stage to cut wall time.

## Summary

**VibeVoice-ASR-BitNet is a real-time, 1.58 GB, CPU-only transcript machine whose context-aware text beats our production chain — but the timestamps everyone assumes are in the box were never in this box, and on long audio it quietly drops the ending.**
