# v41.0: Qwen3.8-Flash-Next 177B Vision on 8 GB: 12/12 OCR With Zero Hallucination — and the Fine Spatial Detail It Actually Misses

> Date: 2026-10-04 · GPU: RTX 5060 Laptop 8GB · CUDA: cu132 · Framework: llama.cpp build 10793 (d230ddd76) + mmproj-F16 · OS: Windows 11 native
> Release: https://github.com/Ultramanmao/ai-pipeline-local/releases/tag/v41.0

**TL;DR:** Our pre-registered expectation said 1-bit quantization would destroy digit OCR and counting first — "those are the easiest places for IQ1_S to fail, score honestly." Measured reality on four controlled-vision tasks: **12/12 table digits read verbatim with zero hallucination, 12/12 circles counted, a 3-field form read perfectly including what the fields mean** — and the *actual* weak spot is fine spatial comparison: a two-image spot-the-difference task scored **2/3 with a repeat-counting hallucination** (one real difference missed, one difference double-counted to fill the answer). The failure signature is precise: **reading and counting are safe; spatial relations between two images are not.** Vision on this stack costs real latency (TTFT 56–153 s for image prompts at ngl8) but runs at generation speeds consistent with the text baseline (3.0–5.3 t/s). Full truth tables, the hallucination log, and the controlled-image methodology (truth JSON locked before asking) included. This closes the 177B side-series started in v37.0.

## 1. The Question

v37.0's base suite included a vision OCR check with zero hallucinated digits — one test, encouraging, not conclusive. A 1-bit quantized 177B multimodal model reading images reliably is a strong claim, and our own benchmark history (the 27B IQ1_S collapse in v15.0/v34.0 material) says aggressive quantization eats *detailed* perception first. So we built a four-task vision probe with programmatically generated images and machine-checkable ground truth, and locked every expected answer before the model saw a single pixel.

The question is not "can it see" but: **which visual abilities survive UD-IQ1_S, and which quietly degrade?**

## 2. Method: Controlled Images, Locked Truth

- **Image generation:** `make_images.py` (Pillow, `uv run --with pillow`) — synthetic test images with exactly known content. No internet images, no ambiguous photos.
- **Truth first:** every correct answer written to `truth.json` *before* any model call. Same anti-hindsight discipline as the pre-registered predictions in v40.0.
- **Server:** `llama-server` + `mmproj-F16.gguf`, `-ngl 8 -t 8 --reasoning off --jinja`, n_slots=4, n_ctx_slot=4096
- **Asking:** per-task `/completion`, temperature per request spec, raw responses retained (`phase3_responses/C_c{1..4}_result.json`)

| # | Task | Ground truth | Why this task |
|---|---|---|---|
| c1 | Digit OCR: 4×3 table | 12 numbers (3–4 digits each) | exact-symbol reading under 1-bit |
| c2 | Object counting: scattered circles | 12 | counting with size/color clutter |
| c3 | Form UI understanding | 3 inputs + button "提交" | structured scene reading |
| c4 | Two-image spot-the-difference | 3 differences | fine spatial comparison across images |

## 4. The Failure Signature (c4 in detail)

The spot-the-difference miss is the most informative result in the set:

- **Missed:** the circle's position shift (x≈300 → 380) — a *spatial relation* change, no appearance change
- **Hallucination:** the extra orange triangle was reported **twice** — once bundled into the "blue circle" entry, once as its own difference — to reach the number 3

This is not random noise. The model detects *what changed* (appearance deltas: colors, added objects) but under-counts *where things are*, then patches the count by double-reporting. A repeat-counting hallucination under answer pressure is exactly the 1-bit degradation fingerprint we expected to find in OCR — it just lives in spatial reasoning instead.

- **Generation is normal:** 3.0–5.3 t/s matches the ngl8 text baseline from v37.0/v40.0. Multimodal does not tax decoding here; it taxes prefill.
- Practical read: vision questions on this box are **1–3 minute interactions**, not chat-speed.

| Pre-registered (2026-09-26) | Measured | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| "Digit OCR and counting are where IQ1_S fails most easily — score honestly" | OCR 12/12 zero-hallucination, counting correct | ❌ **prediction wrong — the model beat our expectation** |

Worth stating plainly: we predicted the wrong failure mode. The pre-registration exists precisely to catch this — without it, the temptation is to quietly rewrite the story as "we expected spatial weakness all along."

For visual diffing/inspection workflows, do not delegate the final judgment to it.

## 8. Honest Limits

- **Synthetic images only.** Clean Pillow-rendered text/graphics is the *best case* for OCR. Real-world scans, motion blur, unusual fonts are untested and likely worse. 12/12 on rendered digits ≠ production OCR.
- **n=1 per task.** One image per task, one run each. No temperature sweeps, no repeat-stability measurement.
- **c4 ground truth was locked, but the task is 3-difference scale.** A 10-difference comparison would likely expose more misses; the repeat-counting pattern suggests answer-shape matching under pressure.
- **Chinese-language answers throughout** (the model answered in the prompt's language). English UI/reading tasks untested here.
- **No adversarial / OCR-hard cases** (distorted text, CAPTCHA-style, low contrast).

## 9. What We Learned

1. **Quantization failure modes are ability-specific, not global.** We went in expecting symbol-level fragility and found spatial-relation fragility instead. Test the ability you plan to *use*, not the one that "feels" most fragile.
2. **A locked truth JSON turns vision tests into arithmetic.** 14/15 is a number, not an opinion — the same discipline as the pre-registered predictions in v40.0.
3. **Repeat-counting is the hallucination shape to watch for** in comparison/diff tasks: the model fills an expected count by duplicating an observed difference. If your pipeline parses "3 differences found" into structured output, verify the entries are distinct.
4. **Multimodal cost on 8 GB is prefill, not decode.** Budget 1–3 min per image question; generation speed stays at the text baseline.
5. **Being wrong on the prediction is a result.** The pre-registration said OCR would fail; it didn't. That upgrades our confidence in the model for exactly the use cases (document digit extraction) we were most worried about.
