**TL;DR:** Qwen-Image 2.1 runs natively on an 8 GB laptop GPU and does two things no other local model in my test set can: **transparent PNG output with correct Chinese characters in a single generation** (no matting, no SAM), and **2048×2048 images in 60 seconds** with a distillation LoRA — a 6.5× speedup over the 25-step baseline that, contrary to my own VRAM prediction, fits just fine. But the path there is a trap: copying the vendor's raw sigma schedule into ComfyUI produces green noise and dropped characters (3/10), and the fix — a stock `BasicScheduler` — is the whole article in one node. Thirteen test runs, one attribution story, and the license caveat you must not skip: **Qwen-Image 2.1 is Research License, not commercial-use.**

## Why Qwen-Image 2.1 on 8 GB

Qwen-Image 2.1 is the successor to the model from my [v5.0 write-up](https://github.com/Ultramanmao/ai-pipeline-local/releases/tag/v5.0) (the 3584-vs-2560 text-encoder trap). This generation ships native RGBA generation: the model emits an alpha channel as part of the image, not as a separate matting pass. On an 8 GB card the interesting questions are:

1. Does transparent output actually work — and does Chinese text survive intact in the same pass?
2. Can a distilled few-step LoRA make 2K practical, and what does it cost in text quality?

Both answers are measured below. The setup that produced them:

| Component | Value |
|---|---|
| Diffusion model | `qwen_image_2.1_int8_convrot.safetensors` (6.76 GiB) |
| Text encoder | `qwen3vl_8b_int8_convrot.safetensors` (8.71 GiB, `CLIPLoader type=qwen_image`) |
| VAE | `qwen_image_2.1_vae_bf16.safetensors` (644 MiB, **must be the official 2.1 bf16 VAE**) |
| ComfyUI | v0.37.0-10 (anything below v0.37 does not recognize the 2.1 architecture) |
| comfy-kitchen | 0.2.35 (0.2.31 throws `int8_linear` errors) |
| Distillation LoRA | Qwen-Image-2.1 Viggle Turbo v0.2 5-step r256 (1.36 GB) |
| GPU | RTX 5060 Laptop 8 GB, ComfyUI `--lowvram` |

## Part 1 — Transparent PNG: six tests

All tests: 25 steps, euler/simple, cfg 4.0, 1024×1024, fixed seed. The trigger format is one sentence of provenance — *"This is an RGBA image with transparency…"* — plus the subject, and **nothing else**. No scene description (test T6 explains why). Transparency was verified with checkerboard compositing and a transparent-pixel ratio; scores are my own plus vision-model checks (subjective, and I say so).

| # | Test | Time | Alpha | Score | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| T1 | Cartoon cat sticker | 85.1s | 71.7% | 8/10 | Clean alpha edges out of the box |
| T2 | Chinese seal stamp "必胜" | 65.0s | 71.0% | **9/10** | **Zero wrong characters** — in seal script, transparent, one pass |
| T3 | Product cutout (ceramic teapot) | 55.0s | 71.0% | 8/10 | Handle/spout edges are product-grade |
| T4 | Photo subject extraction (from reference) | 55.0s | 66.9% | 9.5/10 | Hair strands and clothing edges survive |
| T5 | Dandelion (fine edges) | 55.0s | 59.9% | 9/10 | Every seed visible; semi-transparent fuzz preserved |
| T6 | Sticker **with** a scene in the prompt | 55.0s | ~30% | 8/10 | See below — the "scene kills transparency" assumption is wrong |

**The headline result is T2.** Text-plus-transparency in one generation — a Chinese seal with correctly rendered seal-script characters on a real alpha channel — is something Krea2, Z-Image, Flux.2, and SDXL in my comparison set simply cannot do (all fail on either the alpha or the characters). There is no matting model, no SAM, no post-processing in this pipeline: one `KSampler`, one `SaveImage`.

**T6 is a correction, not a failure.** My working hypothesis was "mention a scene → the model fills the background → transparency dies." What actually happens is more interesting: the model draws the scene *inside* the sticker composition (a scalloped circle containing the cat and the garden) and keeps the outer canvas transparent (~30% transparent pixels). So the accurate limit is: scene prompts don't kill the alpha channel, they consume your composition — the subject-only prompt rule is about controlling layout, not about protecting transparency.

### Transparency: the limits (measured)

- **RGBA at 5 steps degrades edges.** A8 below: real alpha (72.6%) but fringe artifacts and a ragged silhouette — 6/10 vs 8/10 at 25 steps. If edges matter, pay the steps.
- **INT8 quantization hurts fine fur.** The cat came out with knit-texture fur. For fur-critical art, use Flux.2 (see v6.0).
- **Mask + RGBA cannot be combined** in one pass.
- **Output must be PNG/WebP** — JPEG has no alpha channel.

## Part 2 — Few-step acceleration: the 2K surprise and the green-noise trap

The acceleration plan asked three questions. The answers overturned one of my own predictions and produced the best debugging story of this release.

| # | Config | Time | Score | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | 1024², 25 steps (baseline, Chinese poster) | 61.1s | 9/10 | Every character correct across all three text sizes |
| A2 | 1024², 5 steps, vendor raw sigmas | 20.0s | **3/10** | Green noise, subtitle missing 2 characters, small text mush |
| A3 | 2048², 5 steps, vendor raw sigmas | 55.0s | 3/10 | 2K *ran* — but same green noise, worse at 2K |
| A4 | 5 steps + RGBA, vendor raw sigmas | 25.0s | 4/10 | Green noise + ragged edges |
| A5 | 2048², 25 steps (baseline) | 390.3s | 9/10 | **Did not OOM** — my "VRAM won't fit" prediction was wrong |
| A6 | GGUF route | 5s | — | `ComfyUI_GGUF` rejects 2.1 architecture ("Unknown model architecture") |
| A7 | 1024², 5 steps, `BasicScheduler` | 35.0s | **8/10** | Clean image, zero wrong characters |
| A8 | 5 steps + RGBA, `BasicScheduler` | 55.1s | 6/10 | Real alpha (72.6%), fringe artifacts remain |
| A9 | 2048², 5 steps, `BasicScheduler` | 60.1s | 7.5/10 | 1 wrong character in the big title; middle/small text perfect |

### Question 1: does 2K fit in 8 GB? Yes — at every step count

My pre-test estimate said 2048² would OOM. It did not. The 25-step 2K baseline ran to completion in 390.3s under `--lowvram`, and the 5-step version finished in 60.1s. If you have been avoiding 2K on an 8 GB card with this model, don't.

### Question 2: how much do the characters degrade at 5 steps? Less than you'd think — if the scheduler is right

With the correct scheduler, 1024² at 5 steps renders every character correctly (8/10 vs the 25-step 9/10). At 2K, the big title lost 1 character (7.5/10) while subtitle and footer text stayed perfect. **Character degradation from distillation is minor. Character degradation from a wrong sigma schedule is severe** (A2: 2 characters dropped, small text illegible). The scheduler is the variable, not the step count.

### Question 3: what's the combined speedup?

| Resolution | 25 steps | 5 steps (correct) | Speedup |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1024² | 61.1s | 35.0s | 1.75× |
| 2048² | 390.3s | 60.1s | **6.5×** |

The higher the resolution, the more the distillation pays. (A2's headline 3.05× is the trap version — fast and ruined.)

### The trap: raw sigmas don't mean what you think

Here is the attribution story, because it is the most reusable lesson in this release.

The Viggle v0.2 README specifies the 5-step schedule as raw sigma values: `[1.0, 0.875, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25]`, fed to a manual-sigma sampler. In the **diffusers** pipeline those raw values get a resolution-dependent time shift applied internally, which maps the final 0.25 close to zero. **ComfyUI's `ManualSigmas` passes 0.25 through literally** — the sampler ends at a real noise level of 0.25 and never fully denoises. Result: residual noise rendered as green speckle plus dropped and blurred characters. Every run that used the copied sigmas (A2/A3/A4) scored 3–4/10.

The single-variable diagnosis (A7): same INT8 base, same 5 steps, same LoRA, same prompt and seed — swap `ManualSigmas` for a stock `BasicScheduler` (simple, 5 steps). Green noise gone, all characters correct, 8/10. Quantization was ruled out; the sigma semantics were the culprit. (High-confidence mechanism, flagged as hypothesis: I cannot inspect the vendor's internal time-shift implementation.)

**ComfyUI recipe: `BasicScheduler(simple, 5 steps)` → `SamplerCustom`. Don't hand-copy raw sigmas from a diffusers README.**

The vendor's other constraints still apply and were respected: cfg 1.0 (CFG > 2 burns colors), no negative prompt, LoRA scale 1.0, and step count fixed at 5 ("other step counts and CFG do not help"). `shift_terminal` is a non-issue on ComfyUI — grep of `comfy/` shows it is not implemented, equivalent to null, so the diffusers footgun of a default 0.02 terminal shift does not exist here.

## Part 3 — Pitfalls (all verified on this hardware)

1. **ComfyUI < v0.37 does not recognize Qwen-Image 2.1** — silent misbehavior until you upgrade.
2. **comfy-kitchen version mismatch** throws `int8_linear` errors — 0.2.35 in the runtime env is what works.
3. **VAE must be the official 2.1 bf16 VAE.** Using a WanVAE gives `size mismatch`; my models/vae folder still holds the misused `.wan_backup` as evidence.
4. **ComfyUI_GGUF rejects the 2.1 architecture** ("Unknown model architecture") — the GGUF route is currently unavailable for this model (A6).
5. **LightX2V Lightning LoRAs are all for the 2512/2511 generation** — there is no 2.1 build. The only 2.1 distillation LoRAs are Viggle DMD; the official README recommends v0.2 5-step r256 ("no reason to prefer" the v0.1 4-step).
6. **RGBA triggers: describe the subject only.** Scene prompts eat the composition (T6).
7. **Mask and RGBA cannot be combined** in one generation.
8. **Transparent output requires PNG/WebP** — JPEG discards the alpha channel.

## Limitations

- **Research License — no commercial use.** This is the most important limitation of the release and it applies to every result above.
- **INT8 + fine fur = knit texture.** Fur-critical subjects need a different model.
- **5-step RGBA edges degrade** (6/10 with fringe artifacts vs 8/10 at 25 steps).
- **2K at 5 steps drops characters occasionally** — 1 wrong character in the big title out of 3 text blocks.
- **T4 reference-image caveat:** the extraction output showed clothing that does not match my reference photo description. The cutout quality conclusion stands, but I have not resolved the discrepancy — treat the "vs SAM" comparison as subjective and unverified on that point.
- **The raw-sigma mechanism explanation is a hypothesis** with strong single-variable evidence, not a source-level proof.
