# v36.0: MTP Speculative Decoding on 8 GB — 45 Runs, +19% to −26%, Acceptance Rate Is Everything

> Date: 2026-09-29 · GPU: RTX 5060 Laptop 8GB · CUDA: cu132 · Framework: llama.cpp build 10793 (d230ddd76) · OS: Windows 11 native
> Release: https://github.com/Ultramanmao/ai-pipeline-local/releases/tag/v36.0

**TL;DR:** We ran MTP (Multi-Token Prediction) speculative decoding on an offloaded Qwen3.6-35B-A3B MoE across 45 timed runs and 45 quality-checked outputs. The official "1.5–2×" does not survive contact with an 8 GB laptop. What actually predicts the win is the draft **acceptance rate**: mechanical text (math, translation) gains +7–19%, free-form Chinese creative writing loses −11 to −26%. If you enable MTP at all, use `--spec-draft-n-max 1`, leave it off by default — and know that quality is provably unaffected either way.

---

## 1. The Question

In v23.0 we reported a **+92% speed boost** from one flag — `--spec-type draft-mtp` — on a dense Qwen3.8-27B, measured with llama-bench. That result was real, but it was a synthetic fixed-token benchmark (tg64, r=3) on a dense model. Vendors quote 1.3–1.7× (Qwen) to 1.5–2× (general MTP claims) for MTP. The natural follow-up question: **does the flag pay the bills on a real prompt stream, on a MoE model, on 8 GB?**

Three things changed since v23.0:

1. The model class moved to **MoE** (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, 3B active) — different memory profile, different draft economics.
2. We now have a real MTP-native GGUF (`unsloth/Qwen3.6-35B-A3B-MTP-GGUF`) instead of an ad-hoc setup.
3. We benchmark **real prompts by text type** instead of synthetic token loops — because "speed" and "usable speed on your workload" are different numbers.

This release answers: when does MTP actually pay, when does it hurt, and is quality at risk?

## 2. The Three Configs

| Config | MTP | `--spec-draft-n-max` | Draft head placement | Hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A (control) | off | — | — | Baseline |
| B | on | 2 | CPU (`--spec-draft-ngl 0`) | Official-style aggressive drafting |
| C | on | 1 | CPU (`--spec-draft-ngl 0`) | Minimal-risk drafting |

All three share identical sampling and runtime flags:

```bash
llama-server -m qwen3.6-35b-a3b-mtp-ud-q4_k_m.gguf \
  -ngl 99 --n-cpu-moe 999 --flash-attn on --jinja --reasoning off -c 8192
# B/C add: --spec-type draft-mtp --spec-draft-ngl 0 --spec-draft-n-max {2|1}
```

- temp 0.7 / top_p 0.8 / max_tokens 256, 5 prompt categories × 3 runs = **45 timed runs**.
- `--spec-draft-ngl 0` is not optional conservatism: with GPU placement the draft head **crashed** on this machine (lesson from our 2026-09-26 Qwen3.8 session). On 8 GB there is no room for the MTP head on the GPU, so it runs on CPU.

## 3. Experiment A — Speed: The Acceptance-Rate Tax (45 runs)

| Text type | A off | B on (n=2) | C on (n=1) | B acceptance | C acceptance |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Math reasoning | 32.1 t/s | **38.3 (+19%)** | 37.4 (+17%) | 91% | 95% |
| Translation | 32.0 | **34.3 (+7%)** | 34.9 (+9%) | 72% | 88% |
| Code | 34.6 | 33.8 (−2%) | 33.0 (−5%) | 85% | 88% |
| List continuation | 34.3 | 29.0 (**−16%**) | 33.5 (−2%) | 60% | 75% |
| Chinese creative writing | 33.9 | 25.2 (**−26%**) | 30.3 (−11%) | 47% | 64% |

Every row is the mean of 3 runs. The pattern is not noise:

**MTP is a predictability tax refund.** The draft head proposes 2 tokens; the model verifies them. When text is mechanically predictable (math derivations, translation), 88–95% of drafts are accepted and you bank +7–19%. When text is creative (47–64% acceptance), you pay the full draft-forward cost on CPU for tokens that get thrown away — up to **−26%**.

Acceptance rate and net gain are strongly correlated. That single column predicts the sign of every row.

Three more takeaways:

1. **The official 1.5–2× does not hold here.** The all-text-type ledger comes out break-even to slightly negative. This is the same order as our 2026-09-26 Qwen3.8-Flash-Next measurement (**1.10×**: 7.67/7.00 vs 6.69/6.61 t/s, ~44% acceptance). The mechanism is structural: **the MTP head cannot fit in 8 GB of VRAM**, so it runs on CPU (`--spec-draft-ngl 0`), and each draft forward pass eats the speculative gain. The official numbers assume GPU draft placement (Qwen's own B200 figure: 1.34×).
2. **If you enable it, use n-max 1.** C's worst case is −11% while keeping +9–17% upside. B (n=2) has the higher ceiling (+19%) and a much deeper floor (−26%).
3. **Baseline surprise:** the 35B MoE sustains **32–34 t/s** with 3B active params and CPU expert offload — a useful endorsement of the daily-driver setup on its own.

## 4. Experiment B — Quality: Provably Unaffected (45 outputs, hand-checked)

Speculative decoding is verify-in-the-loop: drafts are accepted only when the target model would have produced the same token. In theory, quality is lossless. We verified rather than assumed — all 45 outputs from A/B/C compared side by side:

- Chinese creative writing: 9/9 complete endings (187–202 chars), consistent style.
- Math: 9/9 correct first answers (60 / 34), all on the correct derivation track.
- Zero cases of garbling, repetition, drift, or style collapse across all categories.
- **No measurable difference between A, B, and C.**

Conclusion: **MTP is not a quality tool.** Turn it on or off for speed reasons only.

## 5. Experiment C — Ability Face-off: 35B MoE vs Its Own Sibling vs 72.5 GB (12 outputs)

Is the MTP-flavored build as capable as the daily-driver Uncensored build? Same questions, same params (temp 0.7 / top_p 0.8 / max_tokens 2048 / reasoning off), graded against pre-registered answer anchors (chickens 23 / rabbits 12, unique answer B, 5 idioms incl. "barking", 韩柳, etc.):

| Dimension | MTP build | Daily Uncensored | Qwen3.8 72.5 GB (09-26) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Code (LRU) | 9 (demo hit 2048-token cap) | 10 (complete + tests) | 10 |
| Translation (5 idioms) | 9 | 9 | 9 |
| Classical Chinese (逍遥游) | 9 | 9 | 9 |
| Math (chickens & rabbits) | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| Logic (three liars) | 10 | 10 (finer enumeration) | 10 |
| Culture (古文运动) | 9 (quotations clean) | 9 (sources exact) | 8 |
| **Total** | **56/60** | **57/60** | **56/60** |

1. 56 vs 57 is noise — **same base model, same capability**. The Uncensored finetune costs nothing in ability, which keeps it as the daily driver.
2. **The 35B-A3B matches the 72.5 GB Qwen3.8 (56–57/60) at ~5× the speed** (32 t/s vs 6.11 t/s).
3. The only point losses came from verbose outputs hitting the token cap — a prompt issue, not a capability issue.

## 6. The Honest Assessment

**What actually improved**

- Math/translation workloads: +7–19% with C, +7–19% with B — real, reproducible across 3 runs each.
- Verification that quality is bit-for-bit safe: 45/45 outputs identical in standing.
- A validated decision rule: **check your acceptance rate before keeping the flag.**

**What didn't improve**

- Overall throughput on a mixed/creative workload: break-even to −26%.
- The headline "1.5–2×": not reproducible on 8 GB hardware with CPU draft placement. Same verdict as our Qwen3.8 1.10× measurement.

**Reconciling with v23.0's +92%:** both numbers are honest, they measure different things. v23.0 was llama-bench's synthetic tg64 loop (fixed 64 tokens, dense 27B, draft on GPU) — the best case for speculation: maximal predictability, zero prompt diversity. This test is a real prompt stream across 5 text types on a MoE with the draft forced onto CPU — the case you actually live in. Synthetic best-case and workload-reality are two different numbers; publish both.

| Real solutions | Feasibility | Effectiveness |
|---|---|---|
| Keep MTP off by default, flip on for mechanical batches | ✅ zero cost | Wins where it pays, avoids the −26% |
| `--spec-draft-n-max 1` | ✅ one flag | Caps downside at −11% |
| GPU draft placement (`--spec-draft-ngl >0`) | ❌ crashes on 8 GB | Would be the official-number prerequisite |
| Wait for llama.cpp GPU-draft stability, retest | 🕐 tracked | Only path to the 1.5× class of gains |

## 8. Pitfalls (all four hit this session)

1. **Merged MTP GGUFs don't name their weights `mtp.*`.** The MTP head is folded into the last block as `blk.40.nextn.*` tensors (eh_proj/enorm/hnorm/shared_head_norm). Scanning tensor names to "verify I downloaded the MTP version" fails — with MTP off, the loader even logs them as "unused tensor -- ignoring". The correct verification: `qwen35moe.nextn_predict_layers=1` in the GGUF KV metadata plus the `blk.40.nextn.*` tensors. We wasted a scare thinking the download was wrong.
2. **Assertion gates can kill a good run.** Our first harness checked logs for "offload/layer" wording; this build doesn't use those words, so it flagged a silent failure and aborted groups B/C after group A's 15 runs had already completed. Fix: gate on `print_timing` output and accept either log evidence or timings evidence for MTP groups; add `load_existing()` resume so completed raw JSONs are never re-run.
3. **max_tokens 2048 is not always enough for ability tests.** Both models hit the ceiling on the coding question (one finished with `finish=length` mid-demo). If you're grading capability, either instruct a length limit or raise the cap — don't grade truncation as incapability.
4. **Test prompts must be archived with the results.** The 09-26 ability questions had been run inline and never saved; this session had to reconstruct them from answer anchors. All 45+12 prompts now live in the raw directory alongside their JSON outputs.

## 9. What We Learned

1. **Acceptance rate predicts the sign of the speedup** — 88–95% acceptance → +7–19%; 47–64% → −11 to −26%. Check the draft acceptance column before keeping `--spec-type draft-mtp` on.
2. **The 8 GB ceiling turns the MTP head into a CPU citizen**, and CPU draft forwards structurally cap the gain at "break-even to small win". Vendor multipliers assume GPU draft placement.
3. **Speculative decoding is quality-neutral by construction** — and now by measurement too (45 outputs).
4. **Synthetic benchmarks and workload benchmarks publish different numbers.** v23.0's +92% and this release's −26%…+19% are both true; they answer different questions. Say which one you're quoting.
5. **35B-A3B MoE at 32–34 t/s with 72.5B-class ability (56/60)** is the real headline for daily use on this hardware.

## 10. What's Next

- Track llama.cpp `--spec-draft-ngl` GPU placement stability; when it stops crashing on sm_120, re-run this exact matrix — GPU draft placement is the only credible path to the official class of gains.
- Apply the acceptance-rate rule to other speculative setups (DFlash, n-gram) on the same 5-category harness.
