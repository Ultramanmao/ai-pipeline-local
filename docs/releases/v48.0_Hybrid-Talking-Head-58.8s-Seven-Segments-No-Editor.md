# v48.0: A 58.8 s Vertical Narrated Video on 8 GB — Seven Segments, No Video Editor, and the Numerals the TTS Kept Misreading

> Date: 2026-11-16 · GPU: RTX 5060 Laptop 8 GB · CPU: AMD Ryzen 7 H 260 · OS: Windows 11
>
> Release: https://github.com/Ultramanmao/ai-pipeline-local/releases/tag/v48.0

**TL;DR:** A production pipeline that turns a written post into a 58.8 s vertical narrated video (1080×1920) with zero video-editor time: an AI host rendered by H3 Audio Drive appears only as "bookends" (opening, two 2–3 s beat cues, closing = 22.4 s total), all information rides on Remotion number cards rendered on CPU (36.4 s), and every hard part is a scripting trick — display text and spoken text must be two separate fields (the TTS misreads Arabic numerals), subtitle offsets must be measured after muxing (never estimated), and a picture-in-picture talking head looks fake and has to go. First full run: ~2 h of mostly GPU wait for a video that shipped to three platforms.

---

## 1. The Goal / Problem

We already turn text/image posts into narrated videos for three platforms (vertical 9:16, Bilibili / WeChat Channels / Xiaohongshu). The slow, free way (v44 in this series) works — one photo, one voice sample, SadTalker lip-sync — but it is a single static talking head with slides bolted on. Viewers bounce.

The goal for this pipeline: **a video that feels produced** — real pacing, real visual variety, numbers that move — while everything stays local on one 8 GB laptop and total hands-on time stays under two hours.

The first attempt (call it v1) put the AI host in a 360 px picture-in-picture window in the corner while cards played full-screen. It looked exactly like what it was: a face glued into a slideshow. The verdict from the person who has to watch every cut: *"the face window is too fake."* No amount of polish rescues a PiP talking head. v2 throws the layout out instead — **the human is the metronome, the cards are the content**:

- The host appears full-screen only at the start, at two 2–3 s "beat" cues between cards, and at the end. Never shrunk into a corner.
- All information moves to full-screen animated number cards (Remotion, CPU-rendered).
- Visual change every 5–8 s; full-screen human beat every 20–30 s.

That one rule — human as metronome, not background wallpaper — is what makes an all-AI video feel paced instead of assembled.

## 2. The Stack / Architecture

| Stage | Tool | Role | Hardware |
|---|---|---|---|
| Script | by hand | Hook-first outline: opening summary → 3 card narrations → reveal ending | — |
| TTS | AuK-Flash (zero-shot voice clone) | Narration per sentence, **dual text fields** (spoken vs display) | GPU, minutes |
| Audio post | `proc_audio.py` (ffmpeg) | Broadcast pacing: silence compression + atempo 1.15 | CPU |
| Host segments | H3 Audio Drive | Talking host video from one reference image + the TTS audio | GPU, 20 steps |
| Framing reference | Qwen-Edit-2511 multi-angle | Face crop → chest-up medium shot (the host's "camera test") | GPU, one shot |
| Number cards | Remotion `TtsCardsV2.tsx` | Animated 1080×1920 number cards, rolling counters | **CPU only** |
| Assembly | `assemble_v2.py` (ffmpeg) | 7-segment concat, measured offsets, review + clean cuts | CPU |
| Subtitles | FunASR timestamps → `gen_karaoke.py` → libass | Word-timed karaoke subtitles burned in | GPU (ASR) + CPU |
| BGM | ffmpeg ducking | Music ducked under narration | CPU |

## 3. Step-by-Step Walkthrough

### 3.1 Write the script as timing, not prose

The 7-segment structure is fixed: **open (host) → card → beat (host) → card → beat (host) → card → close (host)**. The script is written to that shape: a hook line that states the result up front (no "in this video" throat-clearing), one narration batch per card, and a closing reveal. Beat lines are 2–3 s of speech — "Here's the speed part" — nothing else.

For the first run (a TTS comparison post) that shape produced a 58.8 s video from 13 narration sentences.

### 3.2 TTS with two text fields per sentence

Every sentence carries **two texts**, and this is the pipeline's central trick (Section 4):

```python
{
  "tts":     "愤怒情绪，MegaTTS三冲到二点四二倍，但五十三个削波点，声音裂了。",
  "display": "愤怒情绪，MegaTTS3冲到2.42倍，但53个削波点，声音裂了",
}
```

The TTS gets Chinese numerals and spelled-out tokens; the screen gets Arabic numerals matching the rolling card counters. Generation budget per line: `gen_seconds = len(tts) * 0.2 + 1.0` — too short a budget truncates long lines.

### 3.3 Broadcast-style audio processing

Raw TTS reads like poetry recital — long intra-sentence silences, drawn-out tails. `proc_audio.py` fixes pacing in two passes:

1. **Silence compression**: RMS below 0.012 counts as silence; any gap longer than 0.35 s shrinks to 0.15 s (tail kept at 0.2 s).
2. **atempo=1.15** — speed up without pitch shift.

Measured on the 13 first-run lines: **78.86 s raw → 57.17 s processed (1.38×, −27.5% of raw runtime)** — gap compression and the tempo lift each take part of that cut. Speech rate lands at ~4.7 characters/s, which reads as "broadcast" rather than "recital".

### 3.4 Host segments: H3 Audio Drive, framed right

Three render jobs (opening 8 s, both beats in one 5.7 s render later split at 2.62 s, closing 10.7 s) at **512×768, 20 steps, seed 42**, then crop `432:768:40:0` → 9:16.

Framing trap: feeding a face close-up as the reference image renders an **extreme close-up** — the model takes the framing from the reference. The fix has two parts:

- Run the face crop through Qwen-Edit-2511 multi-angle (`--angles 0 --distance "medium shot"`) to synthesize a chest-up reference (face ≈ 40% of frame height).
- Add the framing to the H3 prompt ("chest-up close-up shot").

The generated clip's own audio is discarded: frames are extracted, upscaled 2.5× (432×768 → 1080×1920, ~20 min), and re-encoded with the **clean processed TTS audio** from step 3.3. Restart ComfyUI between H3 renders — after two consecutive generations the renderer gets flaky on this 8 GB card.

### 3.5 Number cards: Remotion, on CPU

`remotion-composer/src/TtsCardsV2.tsx` (from the OpenMontage project): 1080×1920 cards where key numbers roll from 0 to target as the narration reaches them (2.42×, 53 clipping points, 250 s vs 4–11 s). Card duration = narration duration; the render is pure CPU (500 / 250 / 415 frames for the three cards) and never touches the GPU, which stays free for the next H3 job.

One production rule: the number on the card, in the subtitle, and in the spoken audio must say the same value — the spoken form may differ (spelled-out Chinese numerals in the audio), the value may not.

### 3.6 Assemble: measure, don't estimate

`assemble_v2.py` concatenates the seven sections and burns subtitles. The trap: every mux step uses `-shortest`, so the final section length is the *shorter* of the streams — **estimated durations drift from actuals, and subtitle offsets built on estimates slide off the speech**. The fix is three lines:

```python
durs = [dur_of(s) for s in sections]        # measure AFTER muxing
offs = [sum(durs[:i]) for i in range(len(durs))]
```

Measured section durations (ffprobe, first run):

| # | Section | Type | Duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Opening (host) | H3 | 7.55 s |
| 2 | Card 1 (emotion) | Remotion | 15.57 s |
| 3 | Beat 1 | H3 | 2.63 s |
| 4 | Card 2 (speed) | Remotion | 7.72 s |
| 5 | Beat 2 | H3 | 2.47 s |
| 6 | Card 3 (VRAM) | Remotion | 13.07 s |
| 7 | Closing (host) | H3 | 9.73 s |
| | **Total** | | **58.82 s** |

Rows sum to 58.72 s of section streams; the shipped cut muxes to 58.82 s — a ~0.1 s concat-container delta across seven sections, which is exactly why subtitle offsets are computed from measured section durations and not from the planned ones.

The script emits a review cut (segment names + timestamps burned in, for line-by-line feedback) and a clean cut, same pass.

### 3.7 Subtitles and BGM

Subtitles are the through-line of the video, so they get real word-level timing: FunASR runs per line to get word timestamps, `gen_karaoke.py` renders **three-state karaoke** — unread grey, current word glowing gold, read white (font 54). (The first cut used static white subtitles at font 40; the three-state spec landed the next day and is now standard for this pipeline.)

Burn-in uses libass with `PlayResX=1080, PlayResY=1920` — and the usual ffmpeg path-colon trap: filtergraph paths need `C\:/Windows/Fonts` escaping and the SRT path's `:` replaced with `\:`, or the filter silently fails to load the file.

BGM is a final ffmpeg pass with ducking (music drops under narration).

## 4. The Biggest Pitfall: The Numerals

The card says **2.42×**. The voice said something that was not "2.42×". Chinese TTS engines reading Arabic numerals in mixed alphanumeric strings ("MegaTTS3", "8GB", "53", digit ranges with connector words) are unpredictable — digits get read one-by-one, mixed tokens get mangled, and the on-screen rolling counter contradicts the audio. In a video whose entire content is numeric comparisons, wrong numerals are not cosmetic — they are wrong content.

| Approach | Result |
|---|---|
| Arabic numerals in TTS text | Misread digits, mixed tokens mangled (digits read one-by-one, tokens drifting character-by-character) |
| Spell out numbers in display text too | Cards/subtitles read like a legal document; loses alignment with rolling counters |
| **Two text fields per sentence** (spoken = Chinese numerals, display = Arabic) | Consistent audio; cards, subtitles, and speech all say the same value |

The fix is one data-structure change (Section 3.2) and it is worth doing **before** the first TTS run — re-generating narration after cards are timed means re-syncing everything.

Second trap, already covered in 3.6: subtitle offsets must be derived from measured post-mux durations. `-shortest` guarantees your estimates are wrong by at least a frame or two per section — across seven sections that's a subtitle visibly out of step with the mouth.

The H3 and audio rows are measured; the script/TTS/upscale/assembly-bucket rows and the end-to-end budget are the planning times reported before the run — the point of the budget is the shape (GPU-dominated), not stopwatch precision.

H3 render cost is the fixed tax: measured **1.6–2.2 GPU-min per output second** (three jobs above), so the 22.4 s of host footage cost ~45 GPU-min while the 36.4 s of card footage cost ~5 CPU-min. Designing the host *out* of the middle of the video is what makes the two-hour budget work. Long-form variant (~7 min, 10+ cards): 4–5 h, H3-dominated.

## 6. What We Learned

1. **Human as metronome.** Full-screen host at opening/beats/closing only. PiP talking heads read as fake — kill the layout, not the render quality.
2. **Two text fields per sentence.** Spoken text and display text are different data. This one change prevents every numeral mismatch, and it must precede the first TTS run.
3. **Measure after muxing.** `-shortest` makes estimated durations wrong by construction. ffprobe the sections, then compute offsets.
4. **Raw TTS shrinks 27% before it hits the timeline.** Gap compression (0.35 s → 0.15 s) plus atempo 1.15 turn recital into broadcast pacing without touching pitch — two passes sharing that cut, not silence alone.
5. **Reference framing propagates.** A face crop in → extreme close-up out. Synthesize a medium-shot reference (face ~40%) and state the framing in the prompt.
6. **Upscale frames, keep clean audio.** The generated clip's audio is disposable; the TTS track is the master. Remux at assembly.
7. **Restart ComfyUI between H3 renders** on 8 GB. Two consecutive generations is where flakiness starts.
8. **Review cut with burned-in segment names + timestamps** turns "something feels off at the middle" into "SEG3, 00:32" — feedback you can act on.
